# Valence electrons connect atoms to compounds

Valence electrons are the outer electrons most directly involved in bonding and ion formation. For main-group elements, the periodic-table group gives a useful starting point for predicting valence-electron count and common ion charges.

<LessonSection title="2. Why ions form" description="Electron transfer creates oppositely charged ions; attraction holds the solid together.">

A metal often loses valence electrons to form a cation, while a nonmetal often gains electrons to form an anion. The resulting opposite charges attract electrostatically. The electron transfer is one part of the process; the stable ionic solid is an extended arrangement of many ions.

Do not describe a sodium chloride crystal as a collection of discrete NaCl molecules. Its formula gives the simplest ratio of ions in the lattice. In the crystal, each ion interacts with multiple neighboring ions.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection title="3. Write a neutral ionic formula" description="Balance total positive and negative charge, then simplify the ratio.">

The formula of an ionic compound must be electrically neutral overall. Determine each ion's charge, find the smallest whole-number ratio that makes the total charge zero, and use that ratio as the subscripts.
