# Unit 1 Mastery Check

Use this after studying Topics 1.1–1.9. These are original practice questions written to exercise AP Chemistry skills; they are not copied from College Board exams. Try each question before opening its explanation.

<LessonSection title="1. Quantitative reasoning" description="Moles, composition, and isotopes" defaultOpen>

A 5.00 g sample of a compound contains 2.00 g of carbon and 3.00 g of oxygen. Which setup correctly begins determining the empirical formula?

<QuickCheck question="Which comparison is chemically meaningful for finding the empirical formula?" options={["Compare 2.00 g C directly with 3.00 g O","Convert each mass to moles, then divide both mole amounts by the smaller amount","Divide each mass by the total sample mass and use those percentages as subscripts","Use the atomic numbers as subscripts"]} correctIndex={1} explanation="Empirical formulas express simplest mole ratios, not mass ratios. Convert grams to moles before dividing by the smallest mole amount."/>

For a calculation response, show units at every step. For an isotope mixture, use a weighted average: multiply each isotope's fractional abundance by its isotopic mass and add the contributions. The result should fall between the lightest and heaviest isotope masses.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection title="2. Read a representation" description="PES, mass spectra, and electron configurations">

A PES spectrum contains three peaks with relative areas 2:2:6, ordered from highest to lowest binding energy. A defensible interpretation is that the peaks correspond to occupied subshells whose electron populations follow that ratio, provided the axis and electron count are consistent. Peak position reports binding energy; peak area estimates electron count.

<QuickCheck question="Two atoms have the same number of occupied subshells, but one has a higher binding energy for comparable valence electrons. Which explanation is strongest?" options={["Its valence electrons necessarily have a larger mass","Its comparable valence electrons experience stronger net attraction from the nucleus","Its electrons are moving in circular paths","It must have fewer protons"]} correctIndex={1} explanation="For comparable subshells, stronger effective nuclear attraction generally raises binding energy. Check shell, distance, and shielding before comparing unlike peaks."/>

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection title="3. Explain a trend with a model" description="Atomic radius, ionization energy, and Coulombic attraction">

Do not answer a trend question with only “it increases across a period.” A complete explanation identifies the direction of the trend and the particle-level cause: across a period, nuclear charge increases while added electrons enter the same principal energy level, so shielding does not fully cancel the increased attraction. Valence electrons are pulled closer, generally decreasing atomic radius and increasing first ionization energy.

<QuickCheck question="Why is a neutral atom generally larger than its cation?" options={["The cation gains a new occupied shell","The cation has fewer protons","Electron loss reduces electron–electron repulsion and may remove the outer shell, so remaining electrons are held more tightly","Positive ions have larger electron clouds by definition"]} correctIndex={2} explanation="Losing electrons can remove an occupied shell and reduces repulsion among electrons; the remaining electron cloud contracts under nuclear attraction."/>

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection title="4. Free-response habits" description="Show evidence, reasoning, and units">

For every explanation, use a claim–evidence–reasoning structure:

1. **Claim:** state the comparison or conclusion.
2. **Evidence:** quote a value, peak area, trend, or feature of the provided model.
3. **Reasoning:** connect that evidence to nuclear charge, shielding, distance, electron configuration, or mole relationships.
4. **Calculation:** show the equation, substitutions, units, and sensible significant figures.

A correct answer without the requested reasoning may not earn all available points. On AP-style questions, interpret the provided representation rather than assuming it looks exactly like a memorized diagram.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection title="5. Mixed representations and calculations" description="Practice the transfer AP questions require">

A sample of an element contains isotopes with masses 10.0 u and 11.0 u. The lighter isotope has an abundance of 20.0%. The average atomic mass is 10.8 u.

<QuickCheck question="Which expression correctly calculates the average atomic mass?" options={["(0.800)(10.0) + (0.200)(11.0)","(0.200)(10.0) + (0.800)(11.0)","(10.0 + 11.0) / 2","(0.200)(10.0) / (0.800)(11.0)"]} correctIndex={1} explanation="Use fractional abundance as a weight. The heavier isotope is 80.0% abundant, so the weighted average is 10.8 u."/>

A student says the first ionization energy decreases from one element to the next across a period because the number of electrons increases. A strong correction should note that nuclear charge also increases while added electrons enter the same principal energy level; shielding does not fully offset the greater attraction. The overall trend is an increase, with known exceptions.

**Short free-response prompt:** A PES plot has a small peak at high binding energy and a much larger peak at lower binding energy. State what the relative peak areas indicate and explain why the lower-binding-energy electrons are generally removed more easily. A complete response refers to relative electron count and weaker net attraction for valence electrons.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection title="6. Additional transfer questions" description="More than one representation, one-step recall, or familiar examples">

A sample contains 3.01 × 10²³ atoms of a monatomic element. Use Avogadro's constant, 6.022 × 10²³ entities/mol, to convert the particle count to moles.

<QuickCheck question="Approximately how many moles of atoms are present?" options={["0.250 mol","0.500 mol","2.00 mol","6.02 mol"]} correctIndex={1} explanation="Divide 3.01 × 10²³ atoms by 6.022 × 10²³ atoms/mol to get 0.500 mol."/>

A PES spectrum compares two elements' 3p electrons. The 3p peak for element X occurs at greater binding energy than the 3p peak for element Y.

<QuickCheck question="Assuming the comparison is between comparable subshells, what does the peak position support?" options={["X's 3p electrons are more tightly bound","X must have fewer protons","Y's 3p electrons are closer to its nucleus","Peak position directly gives the number of electrons"]} correctIndex={0} explanation="Greater binding energy means more energy is required to remove those electrons. Peak area, not position, relates to electron count."/>

**Short free-response prompt:** Explain why first ionization energy generally increases across a period, then identify one reason a simple trend prediction may have an exception. A complete answer references increased effective nuclear charge and connects the exception to subshell energy or electron pairing.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection title="7. Additional multiple-choice practice" description="Build fluency across the Unit 1 content map">

<QuickCheck question="A compound contains 4.00 g H and 32.0 g O. Which empirical formula follows from the mole ratio?" options={["HO","H₂O","H₂O₂","H₄O"]} correctIndex={1} explanation="4.00 g H is about 4.00 mol and 32.0 g O is 2.00 mol, giving H:O = 2:1."/>

<QuickCheck question="Approximately what percentage by mass of H₂O is hydrogen? Use H = 1.008 and O = 16.00 g/mol." options={["5.60%","11.2%","50.0%","88.8%"]} correctIndex={1} explanation="Hydrogen contributes 2.016 g per 18.016 g/mol water, or about 11.2% by mass."/>

<QuickCheck question="Which electron configuration represents a neutral magnesium atom in its ground state?" options={["1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s²","1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s¹","1s² 2s² 2p⁵ 3s³","1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p¹"]} correctIndex={0} explanation="Magnesium has 12 electrons; filling in energy order gives [Ne]3s²."/>

<QuickCheck question="Which species has 10 electrons?" options={["Al³⁺","Na","Mg⁺","Cl⁻"]} correctIndex={0} explanation="Al has 13 protons; Al³⁺ has lost three electrons and therefore has 10."/>

<QuickCheck question="Which atom is generally larger, Na or Cl?" options={["Na, because its valence electrons experience less effective nuclear attraction across the same period","Cl, because it has more protons","They must be equal because both are in Period 3","Cl, because it has more occupied principal energy levels"]} correctIndex={0} explanation="Across Period 3, electrons are added to the same principal energy level while nuclear charge increases, so atomic radius generally decreases."/>

<QuickCheck question="Why is the first ionization energy of Mg generally greater than that of Na?" options={["Mg has a lower nuclear charge","Mg's valence electrons experience a stronger net attraction to the nucleus","Mg has more occupied shells than Na","Mg has no electron–electron repulsion"]} correctIndex={1} explanation="Across the period, increased effective nuclear charge generally makes valence electrons harder to remove."/>

<QuickCheck question="A mass spectrum has peaks at m/z 20 and 22 with relative abundances 75% and 25%. What is the weighted average mass, assuming singly charged isotopes?" options={["20.0 u","20.5 u","21.0 u","22.0 u"]} correctIndex={1} explanation="(0.75)(20) + (0.25)(22) = 20.5 u."/>

<QuickCheck question="According to Coulombic reasoning, which change increases the magnitude of attraction between two oppositely charged particles?" options={["Increase their separation","Decrease the magnitude of one charge","Decrease their separation","Make both charges zero"]} correctIndex={2} explanation="Electrostatic attraction grows as the separation distance decreases, with other factors held constant."/>

<QuickCheck question="A PES peak moves to higher binding energy for comparable valence electrons. What changed in the interpretation?" options={["Those electrons are more tightly bound","There must be fewer electrons in the subshell","The peak area must be larger","The nucleus contains fewer protons"]} correctIndex={0} explanation="Peak position indicates binding energy; a higher value means more energy is needed to remove the electrons."/>

**Short free-response prompt:** An element has two isotopes, X-10 and X-11, with X-11 more abundant. Explain why the average atomic mass is closer to 11 u, and describe how changing the abundance of X-10 while keeping the isotope masses fixed would affect the average. Full credit requires weighted-average reasoning, not simply saying “because it is heavier.”

</LessonSection>
