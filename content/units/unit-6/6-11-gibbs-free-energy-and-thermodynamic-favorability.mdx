At constant temperature and pressure, Gibbs free-energy change combines enthalpy and entropy:

\[
\Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta S
\]

For the process as written, ΔG < 0 indicates thermodynamic favorability in the forward direction under the stated conditions; ΔG > 0 indicates the reverse direction is favored; ΔG = 0 describes equilibrium. Favorability does not tell you how fast the process occurs.

<LessonSection title="1. Track signs and units" description="The temperature term can change the result" defaultOpen>

Use temperature in kelvin. Enthalpy and the product TΔS must have the same energy units. If ΔH is in kJ/mol and ΔS is in J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹, convert one so the units agree before subtracting.

| ΔH | ΔS | Temperature implication |
|---|---|---|
| Negative | Positive | ΔG is negative at all positive temperatures |
| Positive | Negative | ΔG is positive at all positive temperatures |
| Negative | Negative | Favorability is favored at lower temperature |
| Positive | Positive | Favorability is favored at higher temperature |

<LessonSection title="2. Calculate ΔG and find a threshold temperature" description="Set ΔG equal to zero only when asked for the boundary">

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: free energy">

At 298 K, a process has ΔH = −35.0 kJ/mol and ΔS = −80.0 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹. Convert entropy: −0.0800 kJ mol⁻¹ K⁻¹. Then ΔG = −35.0 − 298(−0.0800) = −35.0 + 23.84 = **−11.2 kJ/mol**. It is favorable at 298 K despite the negative entropy change.

</WorkedExample>

When ΔH and ΔS have the same sign, the threshold occurs where ΔG = 0, so T = ΔH/ΔS. Use consistent units and check that the resulting temperature is physically meaningful and positive for the situation.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection title="3. Do not confuse favorability with kinetics" description="Thermodynamics and reaction rate answer different questions">

A negative ΔG means the forward process is thermodynamically favored under the stated conditions. A reaction may still proceed slowly because its activation-energy barrier is large. A catalyst can increase the rate by lowering the activation barrier, but it does not change ΔG for the overall reaction at the same conditions.

</LessonSection>
