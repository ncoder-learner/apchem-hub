A heating curve has sloped regions where temperature changes and flat regions where a phase change occurs at constant temperature (for a pure substance at a fixed pressure). During a phase change, energy changes the arrangement and attractions among particles rather than raising their average kinetic energy.

<LessonSection title="1. Use the equation that matches the process" description="Sensible heat versus latent heat" defaultOpen>

For a temperature change within one phase, use q = mcΔT. For a phase change at constant temperature, use q = nΔH_phase or q = mL when the appropriate molar or mass-specific phase-change quantity is given.

- Melting and vaporization absorb heat: q > 0 for the substance.
- Freezing and condensation release heat: q < 0 for the substance.
- During boiling, temperature stays constant while liquid becomes gas; added energy is not used to increase temperature.

<LessonSection title="2. Break a heating curve into steps" description="Add the energy for each region">

For a sample heated from solid below its melting point to gas above its boiling point, calculate the energy for each segment separately: warm the solid with mc_sΔT, melt it with nΔH_fus, warm the liquid with mc_lΔT, vaporize it with nΔH_vap, and warm the gas with mc_gΔT. The total heat is the sum of the signed energy changes.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: energy to melt">

Melting 0.50 mol of a solid with ΔH_fus = 6.0 kJ/mol requires q = nΔH_fus = (0.50 mol)(6.0 kJ/mol) = **+3.0 kJ**. No ΔT term is used for the melting plateau because the temperature is constant during the phase change.

</WorkedExample>

<LessonSection title="3. Read phase diagrams and curves carefully" description="Temperature, phase, and energy are different variables">

A flat segment of a heating curve does not mean energy transfer stops. It means temperature is constant while the phase fraction changes. On a phase diagram, pressure and temperature determine the stable phase; crossing a phase boundary changes the phase. Do not use q = mcΔT across a phase change unless the problem explicitly separates the phases into individual temperature-change steps.

</LessonSection>

## Readiness check

Identify every temperature-change and phase-change segment before calculating. Use q = mcΔT for sloped regions and q = nΔH_phase for plateaus.
