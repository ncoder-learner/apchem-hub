Calorimetry measures energy transfer by tracking temperature change. The central relationship for a material that does not change phase is q = mcΔT, where m is mass, c is specific heat capacity, and ΔT = T_final − T_initial. Use the mass and heat capacity of the material whose temperature was measured.

<LessonSection title="1. Choose the correct heat equation" description="Specific heat and total heat capacity are different" defaultOpen>

- q = mcΔT uses specific heat c, usually in J g⁻¹ °C⁻¹.
- q = CΔT uses total heat capacity C, usually in J °C⁻¹.
- ΔT can be measured in °C or K because a temperature difference has the same numerical size in both units.

A positive ΔT means the material warmed and q is positive. A negative ΔT means it cooled and q is negative.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection title="2. Move from solution heat to reaction enthalpy" description="The reaction and the measured solution have opposite heat signs">

For a coffee-cup calorimeter, the solution is often treated as the surroundings. If the solution gains 836 J, the reaction loses approximately 836 J: q_rxn ≈ −q_solution. At constant pressure, q_rxn corresponds to the reaction enthalpy for the amount that reacted. To report molar enthalpy, divide by the moles of reaction as defined by the balanced equation, not automatically by total solution moles.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: molar enthalpy">

A reaction warms a solution by 836 J while 0.0200 mol of the limiting reactant reacts. If that amount corresponds to the reaction extent specified, q_rxn ≈ −836 J. The molar enthalpy is ΔH ≈ (−836 J)/(0.0200 mol) = **−41.8 kJ/mol** after converting joules to kilojoules.

</WorkedExample>

<LessonSection title="3. Check units and assumptions" description="The common errors are usually setup errors">

Before calculating, convert mass to grams if c is in J g⁻¹ °C⁻¹, use ΔT = final − initial, and keep the sign. Convert J to kJ before combining with enthalpies in kJ/mol. If the problem provides a calorimeter constant, include q_cal = C_calΔT in the energy balance.

</LessonSection>
