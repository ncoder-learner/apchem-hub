# The ideal gas law links four measurable quantities

\[
PV=nRT
\]

P is pressure, V is volume, n is amount in moles, T is absolute temperature in kelvin, and R is the gas constant. With P in atm and V in liters, use R = 0.08206 L·atm·mol⁻¹·K⁻¹. Match the value of R to the units in the problem.

<Unit3Visual kind="gas-law" />

<LessonSection title="1. Solve with units" description="A repeatable calculation method" defaultOpen>

**Example:** What volume does 0.500 mol of an ideal gas occupy at 298 K and 1.00 atm?

\[
V=\frac{nRT}{P}=\frac{(0.500)(0.08206)(298)}{1.00}=12.2\text{ L}
\]

1. Write the known variables with units.
2. Convert temperature to kelvin: K = °C + 273.15.
3. Choose R consistent with pressure and volume units.
4. Rearrange algebraically before substituting.
5. Check whether the result is physically reasonable and report units.

<LessonSection title="2. Combined gas relationships" description="Hold the correct variables constant">

For a fixed amount of gas, PV/T is constant. This produces familiar relationships: at constant T, P and V are inversely proportional; at constant P, V is proportional to T; at constant V, P is proportional to T. Always use kelvin for temperature ratios.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection title="3. Density and molar mass" description="Derive, do not memorize blindly">

From n = m/M and PV = nRT, gas density d = m/V can be related to molar mass:

\[
d=\frac{PM}{RT}
\]

Use consistent units. At the same P and T, a gas with larger molar mass has greater density. This relationship assumes ideal behavior.
