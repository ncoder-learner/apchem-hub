# Photon energy determines whether electrons can escape

In the photoelectric effect, light striking a material can eject electrons. The photon energy is E = hν. A material has a work function, the minimum energy required to eject an electron from its surface.

<Unit3Visual kind="photoelectric" />

<LessonSection title="1. Threshold frequency" description="Intensity cannot replace photon energy" defaultOpen>

If photon energy is below the work function, individual photons cannot eject electrons in the basic photoelectric model, even if intensity increases. At or above the threshold frequency, photons can eject electrons; excess photon energy becomes kinetic energy of the emitted electron.

\[
KE_{max}=h\nu-\phi
\]

Here φ is the work function. Increasing frequency above threshold increases the maximum kinetic energy. Increasing intensity at a fixed above-threshold frequency generally increases the number of emitted electrons, not the maximum kinetic energy per electron.

<LessonSection title="2. Graph-based reasoning" description="Interpret kinetic energy versus frequency">

A graph of maximum kinetic energy versus frequency is linear above threshold, with slope h and a frequency-axis intercept at the threshold frequency. A higher work function shifts the threshold to a higher frequency. Explain these features from the equation rather than memorizing the graph shape.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection title="3. Common confusion" description="Frequency and intensity are not interchangeable">

Frequency determines energy per photon. Intensity determines how many photons arrive over a given time and area (for monochromatic light). When explaining experimental results, state which quantity changed and which remained fixed.

</LessonSection>
