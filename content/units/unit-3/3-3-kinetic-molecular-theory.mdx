# Kinetic molecular theory connects particles to gas behavior

The ideal-gas model treats particles as tiny objects in constant random motion. Their collisions with container walls produce pressure. The model assumes particle volume is negligible compared with container volume, attractions between particles are negligible, and collisions are elastic.

<Unit3Visual kind="kinetic" />

<LessonSection title="1. Temperature and average kinetic energy" description="Use kelvin, not Celsius" defaultOpen>

The average translational kinetic energy of ideal-gas particles is proportional to absolute temperature:

\[
\overline{KE}=\frac{3}{2}k_BT
\]

At the same kelvin temperature, all ideal gases have the same average translational kinetic energy. They do **not** have the same average speed: lighter particles move faster on average than heavier particles at the same temperature.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection title="2. Pressure and collisions" description="Predict changes from particle motion">

Pressure results from momentum transfer when particles collide with the walls. At constant volume, increasing temperature raises average speed and collision energy, increasing pressure. At constant temperature and fixed amount of gas, reducing volume causes more frequent wall collisions per unit area and raises pressure.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection title="3. When the ideal model fails" description="Real particles have size and attractions">

Real gases deviate most when particles are crowded together (high pressure) or attractions become important (often lower temperature). The ideal model is generally most useful at relatively low pressure and high temperature. At very low temperatures, attractions can lead to condensation, a behavior the ideal model does not describe.

</LessonSection>
