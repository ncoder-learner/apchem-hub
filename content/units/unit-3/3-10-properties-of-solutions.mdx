# Solution properties depend on composition

Solution properties depend on the identities of the solute and solvent and on how many dissolved particles are present. For many colligative properties, the number of dissolved particles matters more than their chemical identity, provided the model's assumptions are appropriate.

<LessonSection title="1. Particle count and electrolytes" description="Formula units are not always particles" defaultOpen>

When a soluble ionic compound dissociates, one formula unit can produce multiple ions. Ideally, 1 mol CaCl₂ produces 1 mol Ca²⁺ and 2 mol Cl⁻, for 3 mol of dissolved ions per mole of formula units. Real solutions can deviate from this ideal count because ions interact, especially at higher concentrations.

<QuickCheck question="Assuming complete dissociation, how many moles of ions are produced by 0.20 mol AlCl₃?" options={["0.20 mol","0.40 mol","0.60 mol","0.80 mol"]} correctIndex={3} explanation="Each AlCl₃ formula unit produces one Al³⁺ and three Cl⁻ ions: four ions per formula unit, so 0.20 × 4 = 0.80 mol ions."/>

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection title="2. Colligative-property reasoning" description="Connect dissolved particles to a trend">

Dissolved nonvolatile solute particles lower a solvent's vapor pressure relative to the pure solvent at the same temperature. This is connected to boiling-point elevation and freezing-point depression: the solution must reach a higher temperature to boil at a given external pressure, and a lower temperature to freeze.

For dilute ideal solutions, the magnitude of these effects depends on the concentration of dissolved particles. Compare the actual particle count, not just the number of solute formulas written on the bottle.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection title="3. Interpret data carefully" description="State assumptions">

When a question compares solutions, identify which has more dissolved particles per kilogram or liter as required. Account for dissociation and any stated concentration units. Do not assume two 0.10 M solutions have the same particle concentration if one is a nonelectrolyte and the other dissociates into several ions.

</LessonSection>
