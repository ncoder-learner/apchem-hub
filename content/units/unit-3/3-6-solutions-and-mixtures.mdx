# Solutions are mixtures at the particle level

A solution is a homogeneous mixture: its composition is uniform at the scale of observation. The **solute** is the substance dispersed, and the **solvent** is the medium that dissolves it. In aqueous solutions, water is the solvent.

<Unit3Visual kind="solutions" />

<LessonSection title="1. Explain dissolution" description="Particle interactions change during mixing" defaultOpen>

Dissolution involves separating solute particles, making room among solvent particles, and forming solute–solvent interactions. Whether a substance dissolves appreciably depends on the balance of these interactions and on entropy, not simply on whether two substances “look alike.”

For ionic solutes, polar solvent molecules orient around ions. The partially negative end of water points toward cations; the partially positive hydrogen ends orient toward anions. These ion–dipole interactions help stabilize separated ions.

<LessonSection title="2. Concentration in molarity" description="Amount of solute per liter of solution">

Molarity is moles of solute divided by liters of total solution:

\[
M=\frac{n_{solute}}{V_{solution}}
\]

Convert milliliters to liters before dividing. The denominator is the final solution volume, not the volume of solvent initially added.

**Example:** 0.200 mol solute in 0.500 L solution gives M = 0.400 mol/L.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection title="3. Dilution" description="Solute amount remains constant">

For a dilution with no chemical reaction or loss of solute, the amount of dissolved solute stays constant:

\[
M_1V_1=M_2V_2
\]

This relation does not mean concentration stays constant; adding solvent increases volume and lowers concentration. Use matching volume units on both sides.

</LessonSection>
