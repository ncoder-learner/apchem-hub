A saturated solution is in equilibrium with undissolved solid. Adding an ion already present in that equilibrium changes the ion product and usually drives the dissolution equilibrium toward the solid, lowering molar solubility.

<TopicLab unitId={7} topicNumber="7.12" title="Common-Ion Effect" />

<LessonSection title="1. Predict the common-ion effect" description="Added ion shifts the dissolution equilibrium" defaultOpen>

For AgCl(s) ⇌ Ag⁺ + Cl⁻, adding NaCl increases [Cl⁻]. The ion product [Ag⁺][Cl⁻] becomes larger than Ksp immediately after the addition, so some AgCl precipitates until the solution returns to equilibrium. The equilibrium constant does not change at constant temperature.

Precipitation occurs and the equilibrium dissolved amount of AgCl decreases." />

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection title="2. Use Qsp to predict precipitation" description="Compare the ion product with Ksp">

Calculate Qsp using the current ion concentrations and the Ksp expression. If Qsp < Ksp, the solution is unsaturated and more solid can dissolve. If Qsp = Ksp, the solution is saturated at equilibrium. If Qsp > Ksp, precipitation is thermodynamically favored until the ion product falls toward Ksp.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: will a precipitate form?">
A solution has Qsp = 8.0 × 10⁻⁹ while Ksp = 2.0 × 10⁻⁹ for a specified salt. Because Qsp > Ksp, the ion product exceeds the equilibrium value, so precipitation is expected. If Qsp were below Ksp, the solution would be unsaturated.
</WorkedExample>

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection title="4. Predict how a common ion changes solubility" description="The ion product changes immediately; the equilibrium then re-establishes">

A common ion is an ion already present in a solution that also appears in a salt's dissolution equilibrium. Adding it increases the corresponding ion concentration and makes Qsp exceed Ksp for the undisturbed composition. The net equilibrium shift then favors formation of more solid, decreasing molar solubility compared with pure water under the same temperature conditions.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: calcium fluoride in added fluoride">
For CaF₂(s)⇌ Ca²⁺ + 2F⁻, adding a soluble fluoride salt raises [F⁻]. The equilibrium shifts left, and the equilibrium [Ca²⁺] becomes lower than it would be in pure water. Do not set [F⁻] = 2s if a substantial amount of fluoride was present before any CaF₂ dissolved; include the initial common-ion concentration in the equilibrium expression.

</WorkedExample>

The common-ion effect changes solubility, not the value of Ksp at fixed temperature. Complex-ion formation, pH changes, and nonideal activities can complicate real systems, but the AP-level prediction follows from Qsp versus Ksp and the dissolution stoichiometry.
