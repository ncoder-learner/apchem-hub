The equilibrium constant K describes the composition ratio for a reaction at equilibrium at a specified temperature. The reaction quotient Q uses the same mathematical form but the mixture's **current** concentrations or partial pressures. Comparing them tells which direction the mixture must change to reach equilibrium.

<TopicLab unitId={7} topicNumber="7.3" title="Compare Q and K" />

<LessonSection title="1. Build the expression from the balanced equation" description="Coefficients become exponents" defaultOpen>

For aA(g) + bB(g) ⇌ cC(g) + dD(g), the concentration expression is:

**Qᶜ = [C]ᶜ[D]ᵈ / ([A]ᵃ[B]ᵇ)**

Use the coefficients in the balanced equation as exponents. Pure solids and pure liquids are omitted because their effective activities remain constant. For Kp, use gas partial pressures instead of molar concentrations.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection title="2. Compare Q with K" description="Q = K is the equilibrium condition">

- **Q < K:** the product-to-reactant ratio is too small; net reaction proceeds forward.
- **Q > K:** the product-to-reactant ratio is too large; net reaction proceeds in reverse.
- **Q = K:** the mixture is at equilibrium.

This comparison predicts direction, not how quickly equilibrium is reached.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: determine direction">
For a reaction, Kc = 4.0 and the current Qc = 0.50. Since Qc < Kc, the mixture has too little product relative to the equilibrium ratio. The net reaction proceeds toward products until Qc reaches 4.0.
</WorkedExample>
