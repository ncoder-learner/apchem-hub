A sealed container of water reaches a point where the water level appears constant. Molecules still evaporate and condense, but the two processes occur at equal rates. Chemical equilibrium is dynamic in the same way: reactions continue at the particle level even when macroscopic concentrations stop changing.

<TopicLab unitId={7} topicNumber="7.1" title="Dynamic Equilibrium" />

<LessonSection title="1. Equilibrium means equal rates, not equal amounts" description="Forward and reverse reactions continue" defaultOpen>

For a reversible reaction, reactants form products and products can reform reactants. In a closed system, equilibrium is reached when the **forward reaction rate equals the reverse reaction rate**. Concentrations then remain constant over time.

Equilibrium does not require equal concentrations. A reaction may have mostly products or mostly reactants at equilibrium. The relative amounts depend on the reaction's equilibrium constant and conditions.

<LessonSection title="2. Identify what must remain constant" description="Stable concentrations are evidence of equilibrium">

In a concentration-versus-time graph, reactant concentrations may fall and product concentrations may rise before leveling off. A flat concentration curve indicates no net concentration change; it does not mean particles have stopped reacting.

Equilibrium requires the system's conditions to be stable and the reaction to be reversible. A continuously open system that loses products may not establish the same equilibrium state as a sealed system.

<WorkedExample title="Worked example: interpret a concentration graph">
A graph shows [A] decreasing and [B] increasing. After 40 seconds, both curves become horizontal. The correct interpretation is that the forward and reverse reaction rates have become equal, so neither concentration has a net change. It is not valid to conclude that [A] = [B] unless the graph actually shows equal values.
</WorkedExample>
