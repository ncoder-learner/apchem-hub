Acid–base reactions are proton transfers. A buffer is a mixture containing meaningful amounts of a weak acid and its conjugate base, or a weak base and its conjugate acid. The pair reacts with small additions of strong acid or base, limiting the pH change.

<LessonSection title="1. Predict proton transfer" description="Compare acid–base partners and identify products" defaultOpen>

For a generic acid HA and base B, proton transfer can be represented as HA + B ⇌ A⁻ + BH⁺. The equilibrium favors the side with the weaker acid and weaker base. A useful comparison is the pKa values of HA and BH⁺: equilibrium favors the side with the higher-pKa acid.

<WorkedExample title="Recognize a conjugate pair">
In HF + H₂O ⇌ H₃O⁺ + F⁻, HF donates H⁺ and becomes F⁻; water accepts H⁺ and becomes H₃O⁺. The two conjugate pairs are HF/F⁻ and H₂O/H₃O⁺.
</WorkedExample>

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection title="2. Identify a buffer" description="Both members of the conjugate pair must be present">

A mixture of CH₃COOH and CH₃COO⁻ can act as a buffer. A solution containing only CH₃COOH is not an effective buffer simply because it is a weak acid; it needs a substantial amount of its conjugate base as well. Similarly, NH₃ and NH₄⁺ form a buffer pair.

<QuickCheck question="Which mixture is a buffer?" options={["0.10 M HCl only","0.10 M NaOH only","0.10 M CH₃COOH and 0.10 M CH₃COONa","0.10 M NaCl only"]} correctIndex={2} explanation="The mixture contains a weak acid and its conjugate base in appreciable amounts." />

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection title="3. What happens when strong acid or base is added?" description="Neutralization consumes one member of the buffer pair">

Added H⁺ is consumed by the conjugate base: A⁻ + H₃O⁺ → HA + H₂O. Added OH⁻ is consumed by the weak acid: HA + OH⁻ → A⁻ + H₂O. The buffer composition changes, but the ratio changes much less than the hydronium concentration would in unbuffered water.

For stoichiometric buffer problems, first calculate moles consumed in the neutralization reaction. Only after that reaction should you calculate the new acid/base ratio. Do not apply an equilibrium formula before accounting for the strong acid or base reaction.

</LessonSection>
