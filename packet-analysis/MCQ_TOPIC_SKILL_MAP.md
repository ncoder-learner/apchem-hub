# MCQ Topic and Skill Map (First Pass)

This map is based on extracted question stems from all 21 PDFs. It describes tested skill families rather than reproducing packet questions. The map is **provisional**: the source PDFs contain diagrams, graphs, spectra and tables that must be inspected before final question-to-topic tagging.

## Unit 1 — Atomic structure and composition
- **Topics 1.1–1.3:** relative oxygen mass fraction in compounds; interpreting mass spectra and isotope abundance; empirical and molecular formula reasoning from composition/combustion data; gravimetric precipitation; hydrate percent composition.
- **Topics 1.4–1.6:** mass-percent analysis and impurity inference; ground-state electron configurations; unpaired electrons; cation configurations; photoelectron spectroscopy and binding-energy comparisons.
- **Coverage gap by filename:** Topics 1.7–1.8 are not represented.
- **Instructional opportunities:** interactive mass-spectrum isotope calculator; empirical-formula workbench; orbital filling and ion-configuration visualizer; PES peak-to-subshell matching.

## Unit 2 — Molecular and ionic structure and properties
- **Topics 2.1–2.4:** ionic character and bond polarity; Coulomb's law and lattice energy; potential-energy curves and bond length/strength; alloy structure, substitutional/interstitial particles, and brittleness.
- **Topics 2.5–2.7:** Lewis structures; formal charge; resonance; bond order and bond length; VSEPR geometry and bond angles; molecular polarity; hybridization before/after reaction.
- **Instructional opportunities:** adjustable potential-energy curves; Lewis/resonance editor with formal-charge feedback; 3D geometry/polarity viewer; ionic-crystal layer-shift model.

## Unit 3 — Intermolecular forces, gases, and solutions
- **Topics 3.1–3.2:** intermolecular attractions and their effect on boiling point, vapor pressure and molecular separation; comparing molecular structures; ion–dipole interactions; identifying bonding from macroscopic properties.
- **Topics 3.3–3.6:** gas particle diagrams; ideal-gas relationships and mole comparisons; partial pressure and density; reaction-related pressure changes; molecular speed distributions; deviations from ideality and intermolecular attractions.
- **Topics 3.7–3.13:** molarity and ion concentration; solution preparation/dilution and particle diagrams; saturation; separation by distillation/chromatography; solubility and polarity; IR spectroscopy; photoelectron spectra; spectrophotometry calibration and Beer–Lambert reasoning.
- **Instructional opportunities:** live gas-law graph with adjustable variables; Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution; dilution/molarity simulator; interactive absorbance-vs-concentration graph; chromatography and distillation animations.

## Unit 4 — Chemical reactions
- **Topics 4.1–4.4:** evidence of chemical change; phase change vs reaction; writing net-ionic equations for precipitation; acid ionization; metal displacement and reaction evidence.
- **Topics 4.7–4.9:** classifying reactions; Brønsted–Lowry conjugate acid/base pairs; precipitation and gravimetric yield; oxidation-state changes, redox balancing and electron transfer.
- **Coverage gap by filename:** Topics 4.5–4.6 are not represented.
- **Instructional opportunities:** net-ionic equation builder with spectator-ion removal; oxidation-number tracker; particulate views of precipitation and acid–base transfer.

## Unit 5 — Kinetics
- **Topics 5.1–5.3:** factors affecting rate; interpreting initial-rate tables; rate laws and reaction order; rate constants and units; concentration-versus-time data and integrated-rate-law/graph reasoning.
- **Topics 5.4–5.11:** elementary steps and mechanisms; deriving rate laws from slow steps and intermediates; effective molecular collisions/orientation; temperature and activation-energy distributions; catalysts and potential-energy diagrams; consistency between a proposed mechanism and the observed rate law.
- **Instructional opportunities:** rate-law experiment sandbox; concentration-time plots with first-/second-/zero-order linearization; particle collision/orientation animation; catalyzed vs uncatalyzed energy-profile comparison.

## Unit 6 — Thermochemistry
- **Topics 6.1–6.5:** endothermic/exothermic classification; calorimetry and heat flow; specific heat; metal/water calorimetry; enthalpy changes from solution and neutralization experiments; heating curves and phase-change energy.
- **Topics 6.6–6.9:** reaction enthalpy per mole; limiting-reactant energy calculations; Hess's law; bond-energy estimates; formation/combustion enthalpies; evaluating setup and stoichiometry errors.
- **Instructional opportunities:** calorimetry energy-balance visualizer; heating curve with phase transitions; Hess's-law pathway builder; bond-energy estimator with uncertainty notes.

## Unit 7 — Equilibrium
- **Topics 7.1–7.5:** equilibrium partial pressures and concentrations; ICE-table reasoning; equilibrium composition from initial conditions; equilibrium in coupled acid/base systems.
- **Topics 7.6–7.10:** calculating and comparing Kc/Kp; reaction quotient and direction of shift; particle diagrams at equilibrium; Le Châtelier changes in pressure/volume/concentration; temperature effects on equilibrium constants.
- **Topics 7.11–7.12:** Ksp and molar solubility; common-ion effects; selective precipitation and comparing ionic products to Ksp; changes to saturated solutions.
- **Instructional opportunities:** ICE-table calculator; Q-vs-K direction predictor; pressure/temperature/concentration equilibrium simulator; precipitation threshold explorer.

## Unit 8 — Acids and bases
- **Topics 8.1–8.3:** conjugate acid/base identification; pH/pOH and hydronium/hydroxide conversions; temperature dependence of neutral water; strong-acid/base calculations; weak-acid/base equilibrium, Ka/Kb and percent ionization.
- **Topics 8.4–8.5:** acid/base neutralization and excess-reagent calculations; relative acid/base strength; titration curves; equivalence-point pH; indicator choice and interpretation; buffer/species reasoning around titration.
- **Topics 8.6–8.11:** identifying acidic protons and resonance-stabilized conjugate bases; oxyacid strength trends; indicators; buffer composition and response to added acid/base; Henderson–Hasselbalch-style pH reasoning; buffer capacity; Ksp/solubility changes in acidic solution.
- **Instructional opportunities:** titration curve with draggable equivalence point and indicator ranges; pH/pOH calculator; weak-acid equilibrium table; buffer response simulator showing pH and capacity; acid-base particle diagrams.

## Unit 9 — Thermodynamics and electrochemistry
- **Topics 9.1–9.7:** entropy signs and particle-level interpretation; standard entropy calculations; Gibbs free energy and temperature dependence; thermodynamic favorability; equilibrium constants and ΔG°; Hess's-law combinations of ΔG°.
- **Topics 9.8–9.11:** galvanic-cell direction and salt-bridge ion movement; standard reduction potentials and cell voltage; electrode mass changes; relationships among E°, ΔG° and K; electrolysis of aqueous/molten salts; electroplating and competing electrode processes.
- **Instructional opportunities:** ΔG° = ΔH° − TΔS° temperature slider; spontaneity region graph; galvanic-cell interactive with electron/ion flow; electrolysis product predictor.

## Cross-cutting assessment skills visible in the packets
- Extracting evidence from graphs, tables, particle diagrams, spectra, energy profiles and molecular structures.
- Selecting a scientific model and explaining why alternatives are inconsistent.
- Connecting particle-level behavior to measurable properties.
- Quantitative work with units, significant figures, stoichiometry, logarithms, equilibrium expressions and proportional reasoning.
- Experimental design, interpreting results, and diagnosing sources of error.
